# IMPACT PURPOSE — LEARNER CONFIDENCE, ACCESS AND PASS-RATE SUPPORT

Project: Mzansi Learner Driver — CLLT Prep
Status: LOCKED PROJECT PURPOSE — GITHUB ONLY
Deployment rule: DO NOT DEPLOY UNTIL COMPLETION BUILD IS READY

## 1. EVIDENCE BASELINE

Western Cape Provincial Parliament reported that:
- the CLLT system became operational in the Western Cape on 26 May 2025;
- the overall provincial learner-licence pass rate for 2025/26 was 63%, primarily reflecting centres still using the manual process during rollout;
- the pass rate recorded through the CLLT system during 2025/26 was 17%.

This 17% CLLT result is the key readiness problem the project is designed to help address.

## 2. PRIMARY PURPOSE

Mzansi Learner Driver exists to help prospective learner drivers build the knowledge, understanding and digital-test confidence required for the Computerised Learner's Licence Test.

The app should support:
- individual learners;
- families helping learners prepare;
- community learning centres;
- libraries;
- NGOs and youth programmes;
- Driving Licence Testing Centres and other public-service preparation initiatives where appropriate.

The project is intended to complement government learner-preparation efforts, not replace official testing or claim government endorsement.

## 3. ACCESS MISSION

The design must particularly support learners who may face:
- high mobile-data costs;
- unreliable connectivity;
- limited access to commercial learner-driver preparation products;
- older or lower-cost Android phones;
- limited previous experience with computerised tests;
- language barriers;
- repeated test costs and travel costs if they fail.

Therefore the permanent product controls remain:
- offline-first;
- low-data;
- no login for core learning;
- local progress;
- plain language;
- multilingual architecture;
- ordinary Android phones first;
- repeated practice without immediate repetition;
- explanations for answers, not answer memorisation.

## 4. MULTILINGUAL PURPOSE

English is the source-master for content accuracy.

Afrikaans and Xhosa are learner-access layers intended to improve comprehension and confidence.

Functional translations must remain LANGUAGE_QA_PENDING until competent human language review is complete.

No language may be labelled approved or production-verified before that gate passes.

## 5. CONFIDENCE OUTCOME

A prepared learner should be able to:
- work through the complete study scope offline;
- practise Rules, Signs/Signals/Markings and Controls repeatedly;
- understand why an answer is correct;
- revisit weak areas;
- recognise Code 1 versus Code 2 control differences;
- become familiar with answering on a phone/touchscreen;
- cope with randomised question and answer order;
- approach the official test with greater knowledge confidence and digital confidence.

## 6. INSTITUTIONAL SUPPORT OUTCOME

The app should be simple enough to be used as a preparation resource by:
- DLTC-linked learner support programmes;
- public libraries;
- community centres;
- schools / youth programmes where appropriate;
- training providers and NGOs.

Institutional use must not require a cloud account or constant internet connection for core learner practice.

## 7. IMPACT MEASUREMENT — DO NOT OVERCLAIM

The app must not claim that it increases official pass rates until evidence exists.

Future pilot measurement should compare:
- baseline diagnostic score;
- practice completion;
- weak-area improvement;
- readiness score before test;
- learner confidence before and after practice;
- first-attempt / repeat-attempt status where voluntarily and lawfully collected;
- official test outcome where voluntarily supplied by the learner.

Any institutional pilot should report aggregate, anonymised outcomes only unless a lawful and necessary reason exists to collect personal information.

## 8. SUCCESS DEFINITION

The project is not successful merely because the PWA works.

Success means:
1. the learner can access it cheaply;
2. it works offline;
3. the learner can understand the language used;
4. every material study topic is covered;
5. important topics are practised in multiple ways;
6. explanations build understanding;
7. the learner becomes comfortable with digital question interaction;
8. the learner can identify and improve weak areas;
9. the tool is simple enough for community and public-service support settings;
10. pilot evidence eventually shows improved readiness and, if supported by data, improved official test outcomes.

## 9. LONG-TERM OUTCOME

The long-term ambition is to contribute to movement away from a 17% CLLT pass-rate environment toward substantially stronger learner readiness, safer road-user knowledge and fairer access to mobility opportunities.

This is an impact ambition, not a guaranteed pass-rate claim.
